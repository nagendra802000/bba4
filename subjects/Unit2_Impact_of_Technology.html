UNIT 2: IMPACT OF TECHNOLOGY

Complete Exam Notes

Simple English • Concept-focused • Exam-oriented

1. Introduction & Learning Objectives

Technology has transformed how people live, work, communicate and make choices. It has become essential for business and society. Automation, cloud computing, mobile technology, digital tools and AI have made life more connected, quicker and efficient.

These technologies enable global communication, remote work, online study, better access to healthcare and services.

Problems associated with technology:
  • Digital divide
  • Work-life imbalance
  • Job displacement due to automation
  • Privacy and data security concerns
  • Decrease in in-person interactions

Learning Objectives

  1. Explain significant ways technology influences society and business.
  2. Analyse positive and negative impacts on society and business.
  3. Analyse how process technology improves efficiency, quality and cost control.
  4. Evaluate the role of product technology in enhancing performance, innovation and customer satisfaction.

2. Significant Impacts of Technology

i) Effect on Workplace

How work is done, managed and coordinated has changed dramatically. Computers, corporate software and cloud platforms increase productivity and efficiency. Employees can do jobs more quickly, make fewer mistakes and handle vast amounts of data.

Routine tasks like payroll, inventory management and reporting are increasingly automated. Employees can focus on strategic responsibilities.

Robotic Process Automation (RPA), machine learning and AI perform repetitive, data-intensive tasks with high precision, increasing speed and consistency. Automation creates new jobs requiring digital and analytical skills, but also raises concerns about job displacement.

Email, messaging applications, project management software and video conferencing have enhanced professional communication while facilitating distant and international cooperation.

Overall, technology enhances adaptability, creativity and competitiveness, while requiring ongoing education and efficient management.

ii) Medical Advancement

Technology has significantly advanced healthcare by enhancing patient care, research, diagnosis and treatment.

Medical imaging, robotic surgery and AI-based diagnostics enable early disease diagnosis and more precise clinical judgments, reducing errors and improving patient outcomes.

In medical research, high-performance computing, data analytics, biotechnology and genomics have accelerated the development of new drugs and vaccines.

Wearable technology helps patients track and manage chronic diseases, while telemedicine and digital health platforms expand access to treatment, particularly in rural or disadvantaged regions.

Ongoing issues: Data privacy, digital inequality and ethics.

iii) Global Communication

Technology has revolutionised international communication by eliminating geographical restrictions and facilitating instantaneous contact.

People and organisations communicate in real time using social media, messaging applications, video conferencing and email.

In the workplace, technology enables remote work and cross-border collaboration, allowing companies to operate internationally, reduce costs and exchange information effectively.

In education, digital libraries, online courses and virtual classrooms increase access to education and promote lifelong skills.

Technology also fosters social connections between friends and family living far apart and raises awareness of problems and cultures worldwide.

Negative effects: Excessive reliance on digital communication may decrease in-person interactions and contribute to the spread of false information.

iv) Everyday Distractions

Technology simplifies life and speeds it up, yet it also increases distractions. Smartphones, social media, notifications and digital entertainment can distract people from daily activities and focused work.

Frequent messages and alerts disrupt tasks, lower productivity and cause mental fatigue because people require time to regain focus after each interruption.

Constant digital content can cause stress, anxiety, overload and burnout. Frequent device checking can diminish awareness and impair memory and decision-making.

Excessive screen time can distract from meaningful interactions and lead to emotional disconnection.

Ways to manage distractions:
  • Limit notifications
  • Set screen-time limits
  • Practise mindfulness

v) Privacy and Data Security Concerns

With the rise of digital platforms for communication, shopping, banking and socialising, personal information is increasingly collected, stored and analysed.

Risks include misuse, unauthorised access and surveillance. Personal information such as location, browsing history, financial details and preferences may be collected.

Businesses utilise data for targeted advertising and analytics, potentially infringing privacy. Sharing data with others also raises risks of misuse.

Data breaches and cyberattacks pose significant threats. Hackers may exploit vulnerabilities to access private data, leading to financial loss, identity theft and emotional distress.

Governments have enacted laws to safeguard data and establish cybersecurity regulations. However, rules can lag behind new technology.

Protection measures:
  • Use strong passwords
  • Use secure networks
  • Use privacy settings responsibly
  • Follow ethical and legal safeguards
  • Increase user awareness

vi) Work-Life Imbalance

Technology has blurred employees' personal and professional lives by keeping them connected through computers, cellphones and remote tools.

Although this can increase output, it may also lead to stress or burnout, longer workdays and less personal time.

Constant availability can cause mental exhaustion, decreased motivation and problems in personal and family relationships.

Companies should promote good digital habits, respect personal time and provide flexibility.

3. Impact of Technology on Society

A. Positive Impacts

1. Transformation in Communication

Technology has transformed communication by enabling instant and continuous global interaction. Social media, email, instant messaging and video conferencing allow real-time communication regardless of distance.

It has lowered cultural and physical barriers, strengthened personal relationships, enabled global collaboration and supported remote work. Educational institutions also benefit through virtual discussions, webinars and online communities.

2. Improvements in Healthcare

Technological advancements have improved healthcare delivery and outcomes. AI, digital diagnostic tools, electronic health records and telemedicine improve diagnosis and treatment.

Telemedicine is particularly beneficial for rural and underserved populations. Wearable devices and health applications support continuous monitoring and preventive care.

3. Education and Learning Opportunities

Technology has made education more flexible and accessible. Online courses and virtual classrooms allow students to access educational resources anytime and anywhere.

Personalised learning platforms can tailor content to individual learning styles and speeds. Technology also supports lifelong learning, upskilling and reskilling.

4. Economic Growth and Productivity

Technology drives economic growth and boosts productivity. Digital tools and automation improve business operations, reduce costs and increase efficiency.

The technology sector has created new industries and skilled jobs including software development, data analytics and digital marketing.

Technology also fosters entrepreneurship by reducing entry barriers through online platforms and digital infrastructure.

B. Negative Impacts

1. The Digital Divide

The digital divide refers to unequal access to technology across social, economic and geographic groups.

Urban and wealthy populations may have high-speed internet, digital devices and strong technology skills, while rural and low-income communities may have limited access.

This restricts access to education, jobs, economic opportunities, e-governance, healthcare and online banking.

Bridging the digital divide requires investment in infrastructure, affordable technology and digital skills training.

2. Concerns About Privacy and Security

Large amounts of personal information are collected and stored by websites, applications and digital services.

Cybersecurity threats such as hacking, identity theft and data breaches can result in financial losses and emotional distress.

Stronger regulations, ethical data practices and greater user awareness are important for protecting privacy and security.

3. Social and Mental Health Problems

Excessive use of online platforms can reduce in-person interactions and harm real-life relationships.

Frequent notifications, online comparisons and digital content may increase stress, anxiety and other mental health problems.

Overdependence can shorten attention spans and disrupt healthy habits such as sleep and exercise.

Technology provides connection and support, but overuse demonstrates the importance of balance and mindfulness.

4. Job Losses Because of Automation

Automation and AI can replace regular, repetitive and manual jobs, particularly in manufacturing, retail and logistics.

Automation increases productivity and efficiency but may affect the job security of low- and semi-skilled workers.

It also creates new jobs requiring technical, analytical and problem-solving abilities.

Conclusion: Technology has enhanced economic development, healthcare, education and communication, making life more connected and efficient. However, drawbacks include job losses, the digital divide, privacy concerns, social exclusion and mental health problems. Responsible use requires inclusive legislation, ethical behaviour and digital awareness.

4. Impact of Technology on Business

Important: This section contains 10 major points.

1. Changing the Way Businesses Work

Technology has changed how companies operate and engage with clients. Marketing, sales, banking and customer service have shifted from conventional operations to faster digital processes.

Businesses can operate internationally, make decisions more quickly and adapt to market changes.

2. Lowering of Business Costs

Automation of payroll, accounting, inventory control and recordkeeping significantly reduces business expenses.

Automation reduces mistakes and manual labour. Cloud-based technologies also allow small companies to operate effectively without large IT infrastructure.

3. Improved Communication Systems

Businesses can communicate with customers, employees and suppliers quickly and in real time through email, applications, websites and messaging tools.

Instant feedback, teamwork and decision-making improve productivity, customer satisfaction and overall business performance.

4. Broader Market Reach

Technology enables businesses, particularly small businesses, to reach customers beyond their local areas.

Websites and e-commerce platforms allow companies to sell products and services worldwide.

Digital advertising and online marketing make it easier and cheaper to reach specific customer groups.

5. Outsourcing and Operational Flexibility

Technology enables companies to contract domestic or international suppliers for non-core functions such as customer service, technical support and administration.

Digital technology allows companies to monitor and coordinate work remotely, reduce costs and focus on core competencies.

6. Role of Mobile Technology

Smartphones and tablets allow business owners and employees to run business activities from almost anywhere.

Mobile technology supports sales, marketing, invoicing and customer service.

Mobile commerce has significantly changed how businesses market themselves and interact with customers.

7. Cloud Computing in Business

Cloud computing allows businesses to store data and run software on internet-connected servers.

It is cost-effective, flexible and scalable because businesses do not necessarily need expensive hardware.

Cloud computing also supports remote work and real-time collaboration and enables small and medium businesses to access advanced technology.

8. Changing How People Shop

Internet-connected customers increasingly expect services that are quick, easy and tailored to their needs.

Businesses therefore adopt online engagement, digital marketing and personalised offers.

Customers expect immediate information and smooth digital experiences.

9. Effects on Society and Organisations

Social media and online platforms have made businesses more visible to the public. Customer reviews and online reputation have become important to business success.

Constant connectivity can reduce employee downtime but may also negatively affect work-life balance.

Organisations need responsible management of online presence and technology use.

10. Technology's Bad Effects on Business

Technology improves productivity, reduces expenses, supports automation, data-driven decision-making and communication.

However, overuse can negatively affect small businesses, relationships and staff morale. Technology also creates privacy concerns, cybersecurity risks, digital inequalities and mental health concerns.

Businesses need flexibility, continuous learning and responsible technology adoption.

5. Forms of Technology

Technology drives how businesses manufacture and deliver products and services. Product technology prioritises design, quality and utility, while process technology concentrates on economical and efficient manufacturing.

5.1 Process Technology

Definition: Process technology is the collection of tools, techniques, methods and systems used to plan, run, improve and control industrial and service operations.

Its main goal is to make the process of turning inputs into desired outputs more efficient, productive, consistent and high-quality.

Process technology focuses on HOW work is done rather than the end product.

It helps businesses save money, reduce waste and mistakes and improve workflows.

Elements of Process Technology

1. Design

Design is the crucial first step that determines process effectiveness. It involves outlining process flow, selecting suitable machines and tools and establishing operating conditions such as temperature, pressure and flow rate.

Good design ensures smooth collaboration, minimises material handling, conserves energy and considers raw material properties, production capacity, safety and costs.

2. Optimisation

Optimisation improves processes to achieve better and faster performance. It involves analysing process data to identify issues such as high energy use, material waste and low production.

Statistical analysis, process simulation and software tools may be used to improve productivity and lower costs.

3. Control

Control keeps industrial processes stable and within set limits. Sensors and controllers continuously monitor variables such as temperature, pressure and flow rate.

PLCs and DCS are advanced systems used for real-time monitoring and decision-making.

4. Safety

Safety involves identifying dangers, evaluating risks and taking measures to prevent accidents and limit damage.

Fire protection systems, alarms, emergency shutdown devices and pressure relief valves are examples of safety systems.

Safety checks, employee training and legal compliance are essential.

5. Digitalisation

Digitalisation integrates digital tools into industrial processes. Sensors, data analytics, AI and machine learning can monitor, analyse and improve processes in real time.

Digitalisation enables predictive maintenance, early problem detection, automated decision-making, reduced downtime and remote monitoring.

Digital twins can be used to test changes before implementing them.

6. Sustainability

Sustainability focuses on minimising environmental harm caused by industrial activities.

This includes consuming less energy, generating less waste, reducing pollution, improving recycling and adopting renewable energy.

Sustainable practices can reduce long-term costs and improve environmental responsibility.

7. Modularisation

Modularisation means breaking industrial processes into separate, standardised modules that can be combined, modified or added.

It makes installation more flexible, reduces time and cost and makes scaling easier.

Individual modules can be upgraded or replaced without affecting the entire system.

Process Technology's Role in Business and Industry

1. Enhancing Efficiency

Process technology saves time and effort by automating tasks and streamlining workflows. Monitoring systems help maintain production consistency and reduce human error.

2. Improving Product Quality

Automated systems monitor production parameters and identify deviations from specifications, allowing corrective action.

This supports standardisation, uniformity, customer trust and regulatory compliance.

3. Ensuring Safety and Rules Compliance

Sensors, alarms and automatic shut-offs help protect workers, equipment and the environment.

Process technology also supports compliance with health, safety and environmental regulations.

4. Reducing Costs and Resource Wastage

Better use of materials, energy and labour reduces operating costs. Automation and real-time monitoring reduce mistakes, rework and wasted materials.

Predictive maintenance can reduce downtime and emergency repair costs.

5. Supporting Innovation and Flexibility

Process technology enables businesses to change production lines, add products and modify processes according to market requirements.

Modular and digital systems improve flexibility and continuous improvement.

Examples of Process Technology

  1. Chemical Process Technology: Reactors, distillation columns, mixers, heat exchangers and separators are used to convert raw chemicals into finished products.
  2. Food and Drinks Technology: Pasteurisers, homogenisers, conveyors and bottling lines are used to process and package food and beverages.
  3. Oil and Gas Process Technology: Separation units, distillation towers, pipelines and gas treatment plants support extraction, processing and transportation.
  4. Pharmaceutical Process Technology: Tablet presses, autoclaves, bioreactors and liquid-filling machines support drug and vaccine production.
  5. Water and Wastewater Treatment Technology: Chemical dosing systems, UV disinfection, sedimentation tanks and filtration systems are used to clean water and treat wastewater.

6. Product Technology

Definition: Product technology is the use of science, engineering and design principles to develop, improve and generate new ideas for real-world products.

Process technology focuses on systems and methods used to make products, whereas product technology focuses on product features, performance, quality and usability.

Product technology combines materials, electronics, software and mechanical components to create products that meet consumer needs and industry standards.

Forms of Product Technology

1. Mechanical Product Technology

Focuses on the design and construction of machines, tools, engines and mechanical systems.

It emphasises durability, strength, efficiency and performance. Examples include car engines, industrial robots, machine tools, pumps and mechanical manufacturing parts.

2. Electrical and Electronic Product Technology

Uses electrical systems, electronic components and automation to improve product performance and control.

Examples include smartphones, computers, appliances, medical devices and industrial systems.

Sensors, microprocessors and control systems allow products to respond intelligently to their environment.

3. Material Product Technology

Focuses on developing and using new materials such as lightweight alloys, strong polymers, composites, nanomaterials and biodegradable materials.

These materials can make products stronger, lighter, more durable, efficient and environmentally friendly.

4. Software-based Product Technology

Involves integrating software, applications and embedded systems into products.

Examples include smart home devices, fitness trackers, automotive software and industrial automation systems.

Software enables customisation, real-time monitoring, data analysis and remote control.

Integration Point: Modern products often combine mechanical components, electronic systems, advanced materials and software to create practical, market-ready solutions.

Functions of Product Technology

1. Enhancement of Product Performance and Quality

Product technology makes products work better, last longer and become safer and more reliable.

2. Promotion of Innovation and Market Differentiation

It helps companies generate new ideas, add innovative features and differentiate their products from competitors.

3. Cost Reduction and Efficiency Improvement

Better designs and material choices can reduce waste, resource use, manufacturing costs and maintenance costs.

4. Improvement of Customer Satisfaction

Product technology makes products easier to use, more reliable, useful and customisable, improving customer experience.

5. Support for Sustainability and Environmental Responsibility

Product technology supports sustainable development through environmentally friendly materials, energy-efficient designs and recyclable components.

Examples of Product Technology

  1. Automotive Industry: Hybrid engines, electric vehicles and self-driving systems.
  2. Consumer Electronics: Smartphones, laptops, smart TVs and IoT devices.
  3. Healthcare and Medical Devices: MRI machines, insulin pumps, robotic surgery tools and wearable health trackers.
  4. Packaging and Industrial Products: Biodegradable packaging, CNC machines, precision tools and advanced sensors.

7. Difference Between Product and Process Technology

Basis Product Technology Process Technology
Meaning Design, development and improvement of products to enhance features, quality and performance. Methods, techniques and systems used to produce goods/services efficiently and safely.
Focus WHAT is produced — final product and its characteristics. HOW it is produced — production method and workflow.
Objective Create innovative, high-quality, customer-oriented products. Improve efficiency, productivity, cost control and quality.
Key Elements Product design, materials, functionality, aesthetics, usability. Process design, optimisation, automation, control and safety.
Examples Smartphones, electric cars, medical devices, smart appliances. Automated assembly lines, chemical processing, CNC machining, oil refining.
Technology Used Mechanical, electronic, material and software technologies. Automation, sensors, control systems, data analytics and industrial software.
Impact on Cost May initially increase cost due to R&D but adds value. Reduces production costs through efficiency, waste reduction and automation.
Customer Orientation Directly influences customer satisfaction and market demand. Indirectly affects customers through consistency, quality and price.
Role in Competition Helps differentiate products and build brand value. Helps remain competitive through operational excellence.
Industry Application Manufacturing, electronics, healthcare, automotive, consumer goods. Manufacturing plants, chemical, oil and gas, food processing, pharmaceutical industries.
Both are essential. Process technology guarantees effective, safe and economical manufacturing, while product technology enhances design, utility and appeal. Together they promote quality, innovation and competitiveness.

8. Social Impacts of Technology

Businesses cannot function in isolation. Social networks and digital platforms are growing rapidly, connecting individuals worldwide and removing barriers based on geography and background.

Consumers increasingly share experiences through social media, internet reviews and rating websites. Even minor errors may attract public criticism.

Negative experiences are easy to share online, and online reviews often persist for a long time. Damaged digital reputation can harm credibility and customer trust.

1. Decreased Downtime

Constant technological connectivity can interfere with relaxation. Smartphones, computers and tablets blur the line between work and personal life because emails, messages and alerts can interrupt leisure and vacation time.

Constant connectedness can make it difficult to maintain healthy work-life balance and may lead to stress and mental exhaustion.

2. Threats to Existing Businesses

Traditional business structures face pressure from rapid technological advancement, particularly in professional services.

Businesses need to determine how effectively they can adopt new technology. Larger companies may use technology to increase market share, while mid-sized businesses may struggle with limited resources.

Businesses that are reluctant to embrace innovation may fall behind, miss opportunities and eventually decline.

3. Technology's Negative Impact on Business

Businesses benefit from faster information access, international markets, improved communication and e-commerce.

However, dependence on technology creates risks such as system failures, cybersecurity concerns, rapid obsolescence of skills and intense online competition.

Businesses must balance technological benefits and risks for long-term success.

4. Relationships in Business

Internet-based tools such as video conferencing, messaging applications and collaboration software allow businesses to connect across distances.

However, virtual communication may lack body language, informal conversations and spontaneous ideas. This can affect trust, creativity and relationship development.

Businesses therefore need to balance digital communication with interpersonal contact.

5. Morale of Employees

Workplace surveillance software can negatively affect motivation and employee morale.

Excessive monitoring may make employees feel constantly watched, increasing stress and reducing engagement.

Respect, trust and responsible autonomy can support morale and output.

6. Time-Wasting Spam

Spam consists of unwanted emails sent to large numbers of recipients. It can divert attention from meaningful work and reduce productivity.

Spam filters can sometimes misroute valid emails and create additional administrative work.

7. Brick-and-Mortar Businesses

Traditional retailers and small businesses have faced increased competition from online shopping.

Online shops offer convenience, greater variety, competitive pricing and home delivery.

Many conventional businesses have lost customers and revenue because they lack resources or technology to adapt.

8. Loss of Community Interaction

Traditional stores have historically served as social meeting places. People gathered, communicated and developed friendships in local shops, cafés and markets.

Growth in e-commerce and store closures can reduce opportunities for in-person interaction and weaken community ties.

9. Changes in the Way People Work and Social Stability

Technology-driven e-commerce has changed employment and society. Physical store closures may affect retail workers, small business owners and support staff.

Technology creates jobs in logistics, IT and digital marketing, but these positions may require specialised skills that displaced workers do not possess.

This mismatch can result in unemployment or underemployment and may create social and economic stress.

10. Loss of Local Identity and Cultural Homogenisation

Physical stores often reflect local culture, traditions and preferences. Small businesses may select products based on regional tastes and community values.

Global e-commerce platforms can make consumer experiences more similar across locations.

Store closures may reduce cultural diversity and local identity as global brands become more dominant.

9. Summary

Modern life and business have been fundamentally impacted by technology, changing how people interact, work, live and make choices.

Digital platforms, mobile devices, automation and data analytics have improved productivity, connected people globally, enabled online learning and remote collaboration and made services easier to access.

Technology has also improved communication, education, healthcare and economic development.

Major challenges:
  • Digital divide
  • Mental health problems from excessive technology use
  • Cybersecurity risks
  • Privacy concerns
  • Pressure on conventional retail
  • Employment threats from automation and AI

A well-rounded, ethical and well-managed strategy is necessary to maximise benefits and reduce risks.

10. Glossary

1. Technology: Application of scientific knowledge, tools and techniques to solve problems and improve processes, products and services.
2. Automation: Use of machines and software to perform tasks with minimal human intervention.
3. Digital Divide: Gap between individuals or communities that have access to digital technologies and those that do not.
4. Process Technology: Methods, tools and systems used to convert inputs into outputs efficiently and safely.
5. Product Technology: Application of engineering and design principles to develop and improve product features, quality and performance.
6. Telemedicine: Delivery of healthcare services remotely using digital communication technologies.
7. Cloud Computing: Use of internet-based servers to store data and run software applications instead of local systems.
8. Cybersecurity: Protection of digital systems, networks and data from unauthorised access and cyber threats.
9. Work-Life Balance: Ability to maintain a healthy balance between professional responsibilities and personal life.
10. Global Connectivity: Ability of people and organisations to communicate and collaborate worldwide through digital technologies.
11. E-commerce: Buying and selling of goods and services through online platforms.
12. Data Analytics: Process of examining data to identify patterns and trends and support decision-making.
13. Digital Reputation: Public perception of an organisation or individual based on online presence and activities.
14. Outsourcing: Practice of delegating business activities to external service providers using digital communication tools.
15. Sustainability: Use of technology in a way that supports long-term environmental, social and economic well-being.

11. Case Study: Medium-Sized Retail Company

Story

A medium-sized retail company used digital technologies such as online shopping, digital payments and data analytics to monitor customer preferences and improve operations and customer service.

Process technologies such as cloud communication and computerised inventory management increased productivity while reducing expenses.

The business facilitated remote work, increased consumer reach and expedited order processing.

Problems That Emerged

  • Decreased face-to-face contact
  • Cybersecurity threats
  • Employee stress from continuous connectivity

Management Lesson

Management needs to balance the advantages of technology with employee well-being, ethical behaviour and secure operations.

The objective is sustainable development while preserving productivity, customer satisfaction and a safe, responsible work environment.

Key Learnings

  • Process technology: Efficiency — cloud, inventory systems and remote work.
  • Product technology: Customer experience — online shopping, digital payments and data analytics.
  • Positive impacts: Reach, productivity, customer service and remote work.
  • Negative impacts: Less personal interaction, cybersecurity risk, employee stress and work-life imbalance.
  • Overall lesson: Responsible technology use is required.
