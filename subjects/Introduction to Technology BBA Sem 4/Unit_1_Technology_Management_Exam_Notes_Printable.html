What is this unit about?

Technology is not just machines. It is very important for economic growth, efficiency and social progress. It changes how we make goods, deliver services and use them. Technology includes machines, systems, processes, skills and knowledge that help in decision-making.

Learning Objectives - Write this in exam:

After reading this unit, one should be able to:

1\. Explain concept and meaning of technology and its role in applying knowledge to practical problems.

2\. Analyse evolution and growth of technology and its impact on society and industries.

3\. Apply principles of Management of Technology (MOT) to improve organisational efficiency and innovation.

4\. Evaluate significance of MOT in enhancing productivity, competitiveness and sustainable development.

PAGE 2-3: CONCEPT AND MEANING OF TECHNOLOGY

Origin of word: Techne = Skill, Logos = Knowledge. So Technology = Practical use of knowledge.

3 Definitions you MUST remember for exam:

1\. Simple Definition: Practical use of scientific knowledge to create products and services.

2\. Betz (2011): Technology is defined as the total body of knowledge, products, processes, tools, methods, and systems employed in the creation of goods or in provision of services. It explains how organisations carry out activities.

3\. OECD (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development): Technology refers to collection of knowledge, skills, methods, tools and systems used to produce goods and services. It represents practical way in which work is carried out.

PAGE 4-6: FORMS AND COMPONENTS OF TECHNOLOGY - 7 COMPONENTS - VERY IMPORTANT

This is the heart of the chapter. Figure 2 in book shows this.

Technology is NOT only hardware. It is = Hardware + Software + Human Skills + System

i) Technology as combination of hardware and software:

Most people think tech is just mix of hardware and software. But importance varies by industry. Manufacturing needs more hardware. Consulting, education, healthcare, IT services need more software/knowledge. Machine without knowledge to operate is useless. Knowledge without tools is also useless. Both must work together in planned way.

ii) Hardware Component:

Tangible, visible, touchable. Machines, tools, equipment, manufacturing facilities, instruments that transform inputs into outputs.

Two categories:

a) Production tools: Manufacturing machinery, control systems, industrial equipment used to MAKE goods.
b) Consumer products: Automobiles, computers, TVs, medical equipment that meet user demands.

Factors for success: Design, automation, precision, energy efficiency.

Limitation: Without software support and skilled HR, hardware cannot give productivity.

iii) Software Component:

Non-physical components that allow hardware to be used efficiently. Includes management techniques, information systems, designs, manuals, procedures, processes, knowledge. Productivity is greatly influenced by software that instructs machines.

Example: Two companies with same machines perform differently because of different management, processes, expertise. In biotech, IT, pharma, finance, services - software/knowledge is MORE important than physical assets.

iv) Know-How:

Key element of software. Practical knowledge needed to carry out tasks efficiently. Answers HOW to do something consistently. Includes processes, methods, techniques, abilities honed through practice and experience. Cannot be fully captured in manuals. This is tacit knowledge. Workers acquire through problem-solving, practical experience, instruction. Example: Skilled operator identifies problem by listening to vibration or sound. Builds over time, gives competitive edge that rivals cannot copy easily.

v) Know-Why:

Scientific principles and logic behind systems. Answers WHY approaches function, WHY certain conditions are necessary, WHY materials react in specific ways. Roots in physics, chemistry, engineering, economics. Encourages analysis, creativity, adaptability. Know-How allows regular operations, but Know-Why fuels long-term technical advancement, problem-solving, improvement. Organisations that understand fundamental concepts can improve procedures, reduce waste, create new products, become technical leaders.

vi) Human Skills and Capabilities (Humanware):

Strongly related. Technology systems need trained personnel to be designed, run, maintained, improved. Hardware and software are connected by engineers, technicians, managers, scientists, employees. Without trained personnel, even most sophisticated machinery cannot produce results, yet talented labour may do great things with little funding. So HRD via education, training, skill improvement is most critical aspect. By investing in workforce, orgs can innovate, adopt new tech, develop long-term power.

vii) Technology as a System:

Should not see as separate parts, but as whole system. This system has hardware, software, skilled personnel, organisational structures, infrastructure. Every part works with others, if one part weak, whole system fails.

Example: Advanced machines require proper maintenance system, skilled operators, steady power supply, high-quality inputs, effective management. If any missing, technology doesn't work well. So managing technology means looking at all parts.

viii) Dynamic Nature:

Ratio of software to hardware is changing over time. Knowledge-based elements like software and data becoming increasingly crucial as automation, digitisation, AI advance. Organisations must continuously upgrade techniques, procedures, abilities. So technology is dynamic system requiring constant learning, adaptation, improvement.

PAGE 7-10: TECHNOLOGY AS A RESOURCE FOR DEVELOPMENT - 10 POINTS - 10 MARKS QUESTION

Technology is an artificial resource (man-made). It is very important for improving mental and physical abilities. It enables turning natural resources into goods and services. Natural resources are limited and unevenly distributed, but technology can be developed, improved, modified, shared. So it has become most critical factor in modern economies' growth.

10 Roles - Write all 10 in exam:

1. Transformation of Resources: Main job is turning inputs into outputs. Natural resources like land, minerals, water, energy have little value unless processed with right tech. Technology provides tools, methods, processes to obtain, clean, turn into valuable goods. Example: Crude oil only valuable when refining turns it into petrol, diesel. Agricultural technology turns land and water into food, raw materials, money. So technology connects availability of resources with economic usefulness.

2. Enhancement of Human Capabilities: Improves physical and mental abilities. Machines make people stronger, allow doing things too hard or long by hand. Automation makes work easier, speeds up production. Also improves mental abilities by helping analysis, decision-making, communication, problem-solving. ICT makes access to information, collaboration easier. Boosts overall productivity.

3. Driver of Economic Growth: More critical than natural resources alone in today's economies. Countries with steady growth have done so by innovation, new things, using technology in valuable ways. Money and workers help, but effects minor without new tech. Tech enables economies to produce more with same inputs. Better methods, efficient processes, better design lead to more output, less waste. So key factor for long-term growth.

4. Productivity Improvement: Most important way tech helps development. Productivity = rate at which inputs (labour, capital) turned into outputs. Tech boosts productivity by introducing better machines, processes, ways to organise work. When productive, businesses can lower unit costs, make more products, more money. At national level, productivity growth means more money and better living conditions. Powerful tool for efficiency and competitiveness.

5. Quality Enhancement: Helps by improving quality of goods and services. Advanced production ensures more accurate, consistent, dependable output. Improving quality makes customers happier, reduces rejections and reworks, makes market more accepting. Across healthcare, transportation, communication, quality and accessibility dramatically improved. Better quality makes businesses more competitive, boosts reputation.

6. Cost Efficiency: Makes things cheaper by reducing waste, energy use, labour costs. Automation and process optimisation help get economies of scale and scope. By cutting costs, companies keep making money while offering competitive prices. At macro level, helps keep prices stable, makes goods more affordable, leads to more consumption, higher demand, overall economic growth.

7. Employment Generation: People think tech takes jobs away, but it also creates many jobs. As tech advances, new industries, jobs, skill requirements created. Fields like IT, biotechnology, renewable energy, advanced manufacturing have become significant sources of jobs. Tech also boosts productivity and growth, leading to more investment and economic activity. This growth creates more jobs over time, especially for skilled people. So tech changes way people work instead of just cutting jobs.

8. National Competitiveness: Very important factor in country's competitiveness. Countries good at tech have better chance in global markets. Advanced tech enables companies to generate new ideas, cut costs, improve operations, respond quickly to market changes. Also makes country less reliant on imports and more self-sufficient. Countries investing in R&D, education, skill development build strong technological base that helps industries grow and remain competitive in exports.

9. Wealth Creation: By increasing output, productivity, value added, tech directly helps make money. Innovation creates new products, services, markets, opens more business opportunities. Companies using tech well make more money and profits, helps country's economy. Revenue benefits society by increasing government revenue, enabling more spending on education, healthcare, infrastructure. So essential for social and economic progress.

10. Foundation for Sustainable Development: Without ongoing tech progress, sustained economic growth not possible. Long-term development can't happen just with capital and labour. Needs innovation and innovative use of tech. Supports sustainable development by improving resource use, reducing waste, using less energy. Helps protect environment while enabling growth by encouraging cleaner production and renewable energy. So not just way to grow, but basis for long-term sustainable growth.

Conclusion line for exam: In today's economies, long-term growth depends more on how well technology is created, used, managed than on natural resources alone.

PAGE 11-13: SOCIAL AND ENVIRONMENTAL DIMENSIONS - 7 DIMENSIONS

Technology is not just machines; it is integral part of society's social and environmental fabric. Every technological change affects society and environment, shaping how people live, work, interact.

1. Technology and Social Context: Social values, conventions, institutions both shape and are influenced by technology that operates in social environment. Families, education, governments, cultural customs all affected by how new tech changes how people live, work, communicate, interact. Changed organisational structures, job roles, skill requirements while enabling digital services, remote collaboration, flexible work. Improved accessibility and efficiency of public administration, healthcare, education. Broadly, raised living standards by improving communication, healthcare, transportation, bolstering human development and quality of life.

2. Changes in Work and Employment: Main societal effect. Need for skilled, technical, knowledge-based workers increased while need for physical labour decreased due to automation, mechanisation, digitisation. Although new tech creates industries and opportunities, also renders specific conventional skills outdated, potentially leading to unemployment if people unable to adapt. As result, skill development and ongoing learning have become crucial. Societies must encourage lifelong learning, reskilling, redeployment through efficient labour laws and educational institutions. When properly supported, advancement may lead to sustainable development and inclusive employment.

3. Organisational and Cultural Change: Significantly changes how businesses are run and how people work together. Advanced technologies promote decentralisation, collaboration, knowledge dissemination, diminishing rigid hierarchies. Data, analytics, real-time information becoming increasingly important for decision-making, changing managers' roles. Affects values, lifestyles, buying behaviour. Digital tech changed way people get information, entertainment, services. Made things easier and more connected, but also raised concerns about social isolation, work-life balance, excessive reliance. So need to manage change to keep people together and healthy.

4. Environmental Impact: Intricately connected. Natural resources severely strained by industrial activity, leading to pollution, soil erosion, climate change, depletion of non-renewable resources. Traditional tech depending on fossil fuels and chemical-intensive procedures seriously harmed environment. Effects emphasise need to use tech sensibly and cautiously. Through waste management, pollution prevention, renewable energy, environmental monitoring, tech may also help safeguard environment. When developed and used responsibly, tech may contribute to ecological balance.

5. Technology as Means, Not End: Important principle. Tech should be viewed as means of development, not end in itself. Ultimate purpose is to serve human needs and enhance societal well-being. Progress focusing only on economic output or profit, ignoring social welfare and sustainability, can create serious long-term problems. Blind pursuit may result in social inequality, environmental degradation, ethical concerns. Therefore choices should be guided by social acceptability, moral responsibility, environmental safety. Appropriate technology - suited to local conditions, resource availability, social needs - should be preferred over indiscriminate adoption of advanced but unsuitable tech.

6. Role in Sustainable Development: Goal is to satisfy current demands without endangering future generations' capacity. Tech may either exacerbate environmental issues or help resolve them. Through waste reduction, resource conservation, emission reduction, environmentally friendly tech including renewable energy, cleaner industrial techniques, energy-efficient systems promote sustainable development. By increasing access to electricity, clean water, healthcare, education, tech also fosters social sustainability by lowering inequality. When sustainability incorporated into planning and management, advancement may promote long-term economic development while improving well-being and preservation.

7. Ethical and Policy Dimensions: Raises important ethical and policy issues related to data privacy, surveillance, unequal access, digital divide. Since unequal access can exacerbate social and economic disparities, inclusive development is crucial policy concern. Governments and organisations essential to establish rules and guidelines that promote responsible development. To ensure benefits distributed broadly, management must incorporate ethical principles and social responsibilities.

Conclusion line: Technology has increased productivity, connectivity, living standards, but also led to social tension, job displacement, environmental harm. For long-term inclusive development, it is crucial to reconcile social justice, economic prosperity, ecological sustainability via effective management.

PAGE 14-15: CLASSIFICATION OF TECHNOLOGY - 7 TYPES

1. New Technology: Technology introduced into or used by a company that has significant impact on how products are made or services provided. It doesn't have to be new to world; it's new if particular company using it first time. Example: Creating company website for online marketing or using CAD software instead of hand-drawing. Essential for increasing productivity and maintaining competitive edge.

2. Emerging Technology: Technology that isn't fully commercialised yet but likely to be in about five years. Might only be used in small amounts or for testing right now, but likely to grow quickly. Examples: Genetic engineering, nanotechnology, superconductivity, using Internet instead of traditional PCs. Often creates new industries, shakes up old ones, causes significant changes in society and institutions.

3. High Technology: Advanced, complex technologies used by rapidly changing industries. When organisation has many scientists and engineers, faces rapid technological change, competes primarily through innovation, spends heavily on R&D, it is considered high-tech. Most time R&D spending well above industry average. High-tech companies often have significant room to grow, but highly vulnerable to competition. Sometimes called "super-high" technologies at cutting edge.

4. Low Technology: Technologies widely used in society and industries with stable technological bases. Mostly use manual or semi-automated processes and hire people with little formal education or specialised skills. Not much money spent on R&D, technological change moves slowly. Products usually meet basic human needs like food, clothing, shelter, basic services.

5. Medium Technology: Lies between high and low. Usually refers to mature technologies that are well-known and easier to transfer between countries or businesses. Common for industries like car manufacturing and consumer goods production. Balance between cost, efficiency, flexibility.

6. Appropriate Technology: When technology works well with resources needed to use it in real life, such as infrastructure, skills, money. It's not level (low/medium/high) that makes it appropriate; it's how well it fits situation. Example: Using high-tech tools without trained workers or proper infrastructure may not be helpful. When developing countries acquire tech from industrialised countries, they often face this problem. Choosing right tech better uses workers and makes production more efficient.

7. Codified and Tacit Technology:

  • Codified Technology: Written down or coded in clear way, like engineering drawings, manuals, formulas, computer programs. Makes it easy to keep, share, pass on knowledge.
  • Tacit Technology: Knowledge not formally stated. Comes from experience and stored in minds of people who create or use tech. Apprenticeships, close interactions, observations, demonstrations, practice are common ways to pass on. Codified tells HOW process works, tacit often tells WHY it works way it does. To fully understand technology, need both written and unwritten parts.
PAGE 16-18: EVOLUTION AND GROWTH OF TECHNOLOGY

Meaning: Ongoing, organised process by which technology changes, gets better, grows over time. Technology doesn't just appear out of nowhere in fully developed form. Instead, it changes slowly as people learn, try new things, invent, come up with new ideas. Each step forward builds on what came before, making systems more efficient, reliable, advanced. Shows how quickly people making progress. Keeps getting better as we learn more.

Three core drivers:

a) Gradual and Cumulative Nature: Grows slowly and in stages. Small changes accumulate into significant progress. Technologies before set stage for those followed. Knowledge never lost; continually improved.
b) Role of Learning, Invention, Innovation: Learning is essential - by doing things again, making mistakes, getting better. Invention comes up with new ideas. Innovation takes ideas and makes better by putting into practice. Can be incremental (small changes) or radical (significant breakthroughs). Both help growth and competitiveness.
c) Technology and Changing Societal Needs: Linked to societal and economic changes. As populations grow, economies grow, tech changes to meet new needs. New ways of making things, better services, more efficient systems come about.

9 Stages in Detail:

1. Early Stages: Manual, basic, dependent on experience rather than scientific understanding. Simple tools for farming, hunting, shelter from metal, wood, stone. Mostly human power, learning through practice and observation. Tools like stone axes, spears, digging implements increased productivity but advanced slowly. Creation of ploughs and irrigation boosted farm output, stabilised food supplies, facilitated early communities and permanent settlements. Surplus food allowed division of labour and rise of specialist professions like craftsmen, dealers, builders. Promoted structured societies.

2. Industrial Revolution: Significant turning point. Small-scale manual production gave way to machine-based manufacturing driven by steam, water, eventually electricity. Inventions: power looms, steam engines, railroads, steamships, spinning machines revolutionised textiles, industry, transportation. Mass manufacturing enabled consistent quality, reduced costs, increased productivity. Required standardised procedures, large manufacturing, infrastructure investments. Promoted industrial employment, new labour structures, urbanisation. Increased growth but also brought problems like social inequality and subpar working conditions. Groundwork for modern industrial economies.

3. Growth of Scientific and Engineering-Based Technology: More methodical and grounded in research as scientific understanding grew. Strong theoretical underpinnings for design and operation via physics, chemistry, engineering. Technology now depends on scientific concepts, experimentation, analysis rather than primarily experience and trial-and-error. Production became more efficient, predictable, dependable. Waste reduced, standardisation improved through better design, material selection, accurate output. Know-how (practical) and know-why (scientific) clearly distinguished. Operations supported by know-how, but long-term development by know-why.

4. Era of Mass Production: Next significant phase. Assembly-line techniques enabled producing similar products on large scale using standardised components, ensuring consistent quality and faster output. Specialised equipment and mechanisation significantly lowered manufacturing costs, lowering product prices, opening new markets. Focus switched from individual artistry to speed, volume, efficiency. Introduced new management techniques: production planning, workflow coordination, quality control. Productivity rose dramatically despite limited product choice.

5. Automation and Advanced Manufacturing: Industries embraced automation, computer-controlled devices with minimal human assistance, increasing speed, precision, consistency while reducing errors and manual labour. Across all industrial activities, productivity and resource efficiency improved. Employees transferred from direct production to process control, maintenance, monitoring, supervision. Workers increasingly focusing on problem-solving and decision-making instead of physical tasks. Higher degrees of technological expertise needed. Importance of education, training, ongoing skill development increased.

6. Information and Digital Revolution: Putting knowledge and information at centre of social and economic life. Propelled by computers, software, telecommunications, internet, unlike previous mechanical and energy-based systems. Revolutionised creation, storage, processing, sharing of information, resulting in quicker, more accurate, efficient operations. Data-driven planning and decision-making increased organisational efficiency, while digital connection eliminated time and location constraints. Value of skills, creativity, ongoing education increased. Transformed global commerce, productivity, development, giving rise to knowledge-based economy.

7. Growth through Innovation and Learning: Long-term advancement primarily dependent on innovation and ongoing learning. Two types: Incremental (small changes improving performance) and Radical (significant breakthroughs transforming industries). Both necessary. Innovation enabled by continuous learning, building knowledge through user input, real-world experience, R&D. Organisations may further enhance and adapt tech by using and refining it to acquire insights. Accumulated knowledge enhances technical prowess and fosters continuous innovation. Industries and nations investing in learning systems, talent development, research infrastructure advance more quickly. Strong cooperation between industry, research groups, educational institutions accelerates progress. Cumulative by expanding on preexisting skills.

8. Diffusion and Transfer of Technology: Essential to advancement across businesses, sectors, nations. Advancements seldom localised; disseminated through joint ventures, licensing, trade, FDI, cooperative research. As they spread, boost innovation and production. Transferred tech often needs to be modified to fit regional environmental, social, economic circumstances. Organisations adapt and enhance foreign technologies to accommodate local resources and market demands. This adaptation promotes sustainable growth, enhances learning, develops local capacities. Particularly crucial for emerging economies - enables embracing established technologies rather than building from ground up, accelerates productivity gains, closes gaps. But access alone not enough; must be internalised and digested. Effective absorption requires competent HR, sufficient infrastructure, R&D ability, supporting organisations. Without these, transfer remains restricted.

9. Contemporary Trends: Current era's fast development and growing cross-field convergence. Unlike past when tech evolved separately, current advancements span biotechnology, innovative materials, renewable energy, AI. Main force is fusion of digital, physical, biological systems. Physical improves infrastructure and manufacturing, digital facilitates data-driven decision-making and automation, biological promotes sustainability, healthcare, agriculture. This convergence produces new products and business models, shortens technology life cycles, accelerates progress. Businesses must react swiftly, constantly adapt, invest in learning. Strong emphasis on sustainability, adaptability, ongoing innovation. Innovation must become continuous cultural practice rather than one-time event. Need flexible mechanisms, energy saving, environmental preservation, resource efficiency, regular updates, R&D, skill development.

PAGE 19-22: ROLE AND SIGNIFICANCE OF MANAGEMENT OF TECHNOLOGY (MOT)

Definition of MOT: Essential for businesses today because it links new technology to business strategy. Makes sure new technologies used to make businesses more productive, competitive, environmentally friendly. MOT is process of planning, developing, acquiring, using technology to help organisation achieve its goals. Helps companies quickly adapt to market changes, generate new ideas, make most of resources. Important because helps cut costs, lower risks, add value by using technology in new ways. Essential for long-term growth, innovation, staying ahead.

4.1 ROLE OF MOT - 8 ROLES - MUST WRITE ALL 8 FOR 10 MARKS:

1. Strategic Alignment: Ensures IT efforts directly complement organisation's overarching business objectives. Businesses may prioritise investments that deliver genuine value by aligning technological initiatives with long-term goals like customer satisfaction, market growth, operational efficiency. Without alignment, adoption might become dispersed, expensive, ineffectual. Methodical way to assess new systems to make sure they support corporate objectives. Example: AI-enhanced customer service boosts development by enhancing brand image and loyalty. Constant alignment ensures technology adapts to changing business requirements. Saves waste, optimises profits, converts technology from support role into significant source of competitive advantage.

2. Innovation Facilitation: By fostering atmosphere that encourages research, experimentation, adoption of new technologies, MOT plays crucial role in driving innovation. Boosts R&D efforts, helping businesses develop new products, processes, services that adapt to shifting consumer demands. Identifies technology gaps, assesses potential solutions, facilitates successful deployment. Advances technical knowledge by fostering collaboration with entrepreneurs, research institutes, external partners. Guarantees innovations provide quantifiable corporate value and are implemented in practice. Helps businesses adapt swiftly, get competitive edge, long-term success.

3. Competitive Advantage: Strategically used tech may provide significant competitive edge. Companies may identify and adopt technologies that boost productivity, improve product quality, reduce costs, enhance customer satisfaction by practising good MOT. Digital tools like AI-based recommendations and sophisticated logistics deliver better consumer experiences, while automated manufacturing ensures faster production and consistent quality. MOT enables monitoring tech performance, predicting market trends, responding swiftly to rivals. By integrating tech into strategic decision-making, companies create breakthroughs hard to copy. Tech transforms from support to strategic asset, facilitating differentiation, long-term competitiveness, market leadership.

4. Resource Optimisation: Best possible use of technical resources including people, hardware, software, intellectual property, ensured by effective MOT. Assesses costs, risks, benefits of various technologies to distribute resources where they contribute most value and prevent needless expenditures. Example: Using cloud computing may increase scalability and flexibility while lowering infrastructure expenses. Matches staff competencies with tech requirements, facilitating easier adoption. Also plans for timely updates or replacements, managing technology's lifecycle. Saves waste, improves operational effectiveness, increases returns on investments. Ensures tech stays strategic asset rather than liability.

5. Risk Management: Goal is to identify, evaluate, minimise technology hazards that might interfere with company operations. Hazards: System malfunctions, outdated software, cybersecurity issues, implementation difficulties. To guarantee continuity and security, MOT offers organised strategies including frequent system upgrades, robust cybersecurity controls, data backup plans. Considers long-term effects like market shifts, compatibility, regulatory compliance. Preserves operational stability, lowers monetary losses, safeguards consumer confidence. By facilitating speedy recovery from disruptions and guaranteeing innovation does not jeopardise security, dependability, long-term value, improves resilience.

6. Organisational Learning: Emphasises value of skill development, knowledge management, ongoing adaptation. Fosters collaborative learning culture by encouraging departments to share best practices, lessons learned, successful implementation experiences. To ensure effective use of new technologies like ERP platforms and AI-based analytics tools which promote more efficient operations and improved decision-making, employee training is crucial. Records project and implementation insights, generating institutional memory that fosters further innovation. Organisations maintain flexibility, resilience, ability to adopt new tech successfully through ongoing learning. Guarantees investments enhance competencies, streamline procedures, provide long-term competitive edge.

7. Change Management: Helps businesses transition from antiquated to more modern systems. Workflow, process, employee job changes often necessary when introducing new technology, may encounter opposition. Through effective communication, training, stakeholder participation, MOT offers organised method to limit disruption and guarantee seamless implementation. Example: Employees must adapt decision-making and acquire new technologies when integrating AI-based information into sales forecasting. By resolving issues, fostering trust, assisting users, facilitates efficient planning and management of changes. Continuous improvement, result assessment, ongoing monitoring guarantee effective adoption, long-term productivity, maximum benefit.

8. Sustainability and Social Responsibility: Primary goal is use of environmentally benign, morally sound, legally compliant technology. Organisations under growing pressure from stakeholders to lessen environmental impact and make constructive contributions to society. Supports energy-efficient manufacturing systems and other innovations that minimise waste, reduce hazardous emissions, decrease energy usage. When adopting new tech, also ensures social effects like equitable labour practices, accessibility, community well-being considered. Long-term viability supported, stakeholder expectations met, business reputation improved when sustainability included. By aligning choices with ethical and environmental obligations, businesses gain competitive advantage, greater efficiency, long-term success.

PAGE 27-28: CASE STUDY - Surya Agro Equipments - 10 MARKS FULL ANSWER

Company: Medium-sized manufacturing producing agricultural tools and machinery for rural markets in India. For years relied on traditional production using manual and semi-automatic machines. While this employed local workers, faced challenges: low productivity, inconsistent product quality, rising competition from technologically advanced firms.

Decision: Management decided to introduce new technology in form of computer-controlled machines and basic automation in selected processes. Faced constraints: limited financial resources, lack of highly skilled technicians, inadequate infrastructure. Some employees resisted, fearing job losses and difficulty adapting.

Solution - Appropriate Technology Approach: After careful analysis, opted for appropriate technology. Instead of fully automated systems, introduced medium-level technology that combined improved machinery with worker training programs. Senior technicians encouraged to share practical experience (tacit knowledge) with junior workers through on-the-job training. Over time productivity improved, product defects reduced, employee acceptance increased. Also adopted environmentally friendly practices to minimise waste and energy consumption.

Questions Answers:

1\. Technological problems: Low productivity, inconsistent quality, traditional manual methods, rising competition, outdated production.

2\. Appropriate technology application: Not buying highest tech, but medium-level tech matching financial resources, skill level, infrastructure. Combined machines + training + tacit knowledge sharing. Balanced cost, skills, local needs.

3\. Role of MOT: Improved productivity (better machines), employee acceptance (training, sharing, addressing fear of job loss, on-the-job training), sustainability (eco-friendly practices, less waste, energy). Shows importance of balancing productivity, HR, costs, sustainability. Effective MOT = right tech selection + change management + learning + sustainability.

This case highlights importance of effective technology management in balancing productivity, human resources, costs, and sustainability.
